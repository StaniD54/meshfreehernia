The method

In the Desarda repair, a strip of the patient's own external oblique aponeurosis (a flat tendon sheet of the abdominal wall) is left attached at one end, turned down and sutured to reinforce the back wall of the inguinal canal. The weak area is closed with living tissue that keeps moving with the abdominal wall.

The operation is open, not laparoscopic. No mesh is implanted, so there is no foreign material to become a source of long-term discomfort. Most primary inguinal hernias can be treated this way, including hernias on both sides in one operation. Whether the method suits your hernia is decided at consultation, after examination and a review of your scans.

2012Co-author of the first European randomised trial of Desarda vs the Lichtenstein mesh repair

Szopinski J, Dabrowiecki S, Pierscinski S, Jackowski M, Jaworski M, Szuflet Z. Desarda versus Lichtenstein technique for primary inguinal hernia treatment: 3-year results of a randomized clinical trial. World J Surg 2012;36:984–992. The first European randomised comparison of the two techniques, in 208 male patients. doi:10.1007/s00268-012-1508-1

The trial compared the Desarda repair with the Lichtenstein repair, the most widely used open mesh technique and long regarded as the gold standard of open inguinal hernia surgery. At 3-year follow-up the two gave comparable results: recurrence (two cases in each group), chronic pain, foreign body sensation and return to everyday activity did not differ. Collections of tissue fluid under the wound (seroma) were significantly less frequent after the Desarda repair. The results confirmed the effectiveness of the method and encouraged its routine use.
